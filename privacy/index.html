2. An Important Distinction: Customers and End Users

AlloTalk is sold to businesses. Understanding who controls your data depends on how you came to use it.

Business Customers. The organization that signs up for AlloTalk, agrees to our Terms of Service, and pays for the account. When a Business Customer uses AlloTalk, that organization determines what data is collected, who has access to it, how long it is kept (within the options we provide), and what it is used for. In privacy-law terms, the Business Customer is the controller of that data and Iterum acts as a processor on their behalf.

End Users. The individuals who use AlloTalk under a Business Customer's account — typically employees, contractors, and crew members. In most cases, an End User did not personally sign up for AlloTalk; their employer deployed it.

What this means if you are an End User. Your employer or the organization operating your AlloTalk account can access the voice messages, text messages, and activity associated with your account, subject to the settings they configure. Iterum handles this data according to that organization's instructions and this Policy. If you have questions about why your organization uses AlloTalk, what it retains, or who inside your organization can see your communications, direct those questions to your employer — we cannot answer them on your organization's behalf, and we cannot delete or release your data without your organization's authorization.

Where we act as a processor, requests to access, correct, or delete data should be directed to the Business Customer. We will assist our Business Customers in responding to such requests as described in Section 9 and in our Data Processing Addendum.

Website visitors and direct sign-ups. When you visit our marketing site, contact us, or create an individual account not administered by an organization, Iterum acts as the controller of that information.

3. Information We Collect

3.1 Information You or Your Organization Provide

  • Account information. Name, email address, phone number, job title or role, and organization name.
  • Authentication data. Credentials and session information, managed through our identity provider (see Section 6).
  • Team and organizational structure. Channels, groups, crews, roles, and permissions configured by an account administrator.
  • Billing information. Company name, billing contact, and billing address. Payment card details are collected and stored directly by our payment processor; Iterum does not receive or store full card numbers.
  • Support communications. Messages, attachments, and diagnostic information you send us.

3.2 Content You Transmit Through the Services

  • Voice messages. Audio recordings created when you use push-to-talk.
  • Text messages and attachments, if sent through the Services.
  • Message metadata. Sender, recipients, channel, timestamps, duration, and delivery and playback status.

3.3 Information Collected Automatically

  • Device and technical information. Device model, operating system and version, app version, language, time zone, and mobile network type.
  • Log and diagnostic data. IP address, access timestamps, feature usage, crash reports, and performance metrics.
  • Usage analytics. Aggregate and account-level information about how the Services are used, such as session frequency and feature adoption.

3.4 Information We Do Not Collect

We want to be explicit about this, because voice applications raise reasonable concerns:

  • We do not create voiceprints. We do not extract, derive, or store voiceprints, speaker embeddings, or any other biometric identifier from audio transmitted through the Services.
  • We do not perform speaker identification or voice authentication. We identify who sent a message by the account that sent it, not by analyzing the sound of anyone's voice.
  • We do not perform speech-to-text transcription of voice messages.
  • We do not perform continuous or background audio capture. Audio is captured only while a user actively holds the push-to-talk control or otherwise deliberately initiates a recording.
  • We do not sell personal information, and we do not share it for cross-context behavioral advertising. See Section 10.
  • We do not use customer content to train machine learning models.

3.5 Precise Location

AlloTalk does not collect precise geolocation data. We do not request location permission from your device, and we do not track the location of End Users. Approximate region may be inferred from IP address for security and fraud-prevention purposes only.

4. AlloTalk: Product-Specific Practices

4.1 How Push-to-Talk Recording Works

AlloTalk records audio only while a user is actively transmitting — holding the push-to-talk control or using an equivalent deliberate action. The application provides a clear on-screen indication while recording is active. AlloTalk does not listen in the background and does not capture audio between transmissions.

4.2 Consent to Recording

Voice messages sent through AlloTalk are recorded and stored so that recipients can play them back. By using AlloTalk to send a voice message, you consent to that recording. Because participants in an AlloTalk channel are deliberate participants in a recorded communication, all parties to the communication are on notice that it is recorded.

Business Customers are responsible for ensuring that their End Users are informed that AlloTalk records voice communications and that those recordings may be accessible to the organization. Some jurisdictions — including California, which requires the consent of all parties to the recording of a confidential communication — impose specific requirements on recorded communications. Business Customers are responsible for compliance with the laws applicable to their workforce and operations.

Do not use AlloTalk to record any communication you are not authorized to record.

4.3 Voice Message Retention

Voice messages do not expire automatically. Messages remain available in your organization's workspace until an administrator of that organization deletes them, or until the organization's account is terminated and its data is deleted under Section 8.

This means a voice message you send today may remain accessible to your organization indefinitely. Please keep this in mind when using the Services.

Deleted messages are removed from active storage promptly. Encrypted backups are taken every 24 hours and retained for 7 days, so deleted content may persist in backup form until the last backup containing it ages out.

4.4 Organizational Access

Account administrators designated by a Business Customer may be able to view channel membership, message metadata, and message content within their organization's account, depending on the permissions configured. Iterum does not control these settings and does not determine who within an organization has access.

4.5 Push Notifications

Delivering push notifications requires transmitting a device token and limited notification content through Apple Push Notification service and Google Firebase Cloud Messaging. These providers process this information under their own terms.

Notification previews contain the sender's name and the channel the message was sent to. They do not contain voice message audio or message content. Audio is retrieved from our servers only after you open the application.
